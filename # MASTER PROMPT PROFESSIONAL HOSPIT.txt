- Password reset through a properly secured process.
- Account activation and deactivation.
- Login-attempt monitoring.
- Rate limiting.
- Session expiration or token expiration.
- Appropriate authentication error handling.
- Secure storage and transmission of authentication credentials.

Choose JWT or session-based authentication based on the application architecture. Explain the choice before implementing it.

## Authorization requirements

Create reusable authorization middleware for:

- Authentication.
- Role verification.
- Permission verification.
- Hospital assignment verification.
- Patient ownership verification.
- Resource-level access verification.

Return:
- HTTP 401 when authentication is missing or invalid.
- HTTP 403 when a user is authenticated but lacks permission.
- Appropriate not-found responses when needed to avoid disclosing restricted resource existence.

Never rely on frontend role checks alone.

Never trust a role, user ID, patient ID, or hospital ID supplied by the browser without validating it on the backend.

Do not allow public registration to create Super Admin or other privileged accounts.

# 5. MULTI-HOSPITAL ARCHITECTURE

The system must support multiple hospitals.

For example:
- Hospital A.
- Hospital B.
- Hospital C.

Each hospital must have its own authorized employees, departments, operational records, and financial records.

Use a shared MySQL database with appropriate hospital-specific relationships unless a different architecture is justified.

## Tenant-isolation requirements

- Every hospital-specific operational record must be linked to the correct hospital.
- Hospital employees must only access records permitted for their hospital assignment.
- A Hospital Manager must not automatically access another hospital's private operational information.
- The backend must validate hospital membership and permissions on every relevant request.
- A hospital ID in a URL must never be sufficient to grant access.
- Financial records must remain associated with the correct hospital.
- Hospital-specific reports must not accidentally include another hospital's data.
- Cross-hospital patient information sharing must follow the access-control rules described below.

Design and test the application against insecure direct object reference (IDOR) vulnerabilities and other tenant-isolation failures.

# 7. CRITICAL REQUIREMENT: RETURNING PATIENT HISTORY

**Whenever a previously registered patient returns, the system must retrieve their existing patient information and available historical records rather than treating them as a completely new patient.**

The patient profile must remain connected to the patient's historical records across visits.

When an authorized healthcare professional opens the patient record, the system should display relevant historical information, such as:

- Previous hospital visits.
- Previous appointments.
- Previous diagnoses.
- Clinical notes that the user is authorized to access.
- Recorded allergies.
- Existing medical conditions.
- Previous laboratory requests.
- Previously released laboratory results.
- Prescriptions and medication history.
- Previous treatments.
- Referrals.
- Hospital admission and discharge history, where applicable.
- Follow-up instructions.
- Previous invoices and payments, when the user has financial permissions.

Historical information must be organized by date, hospital, visit, and record type where appropriate.

## Do not overwrite history

When a patient returns:

- Do not replace previous visit information with new information.
- Do not delete old laboratory results when recording new results.
- Do not replace old prescriptions with new prescriptions.
- Do not erase previous diagnoses when a new diagnosis is recorded.
- Do not combine separate visits into one visit.
- Do not create a duplicate patient just because the patient attends another department or returns on another date.

Instead, create a new visit record linked to the existing patient.

For example:

Patient number: `PAT-000123`

Patient profile:
- One patient identity record.

Visit history:
- Visit 1: 5 January.
- Visit 2: 18 February.
- Visit 3: 9 March.

Each visit must contain its own appropriate consultation, laboratory requests, prescriptions, invoices, and other records.

The patient's history must remain available according to the user's permissions and applicable retention rules.

# 8. CROSS-HOSPITAL PATIENT INFORMATION SHARING

The system must support patients who visit more than one hospital.

For example:

1. A patient visits Hospital A.
2. Hospital A registers the patient and records a consultation.
3. The patient undergoes laboratory testing.
4. The patient later visits Hospital B.
5. Hospital B identifies the same patient.
6. If cross-hospital sharing is authorized and legally permitted, the system makes available the relevant shared patient history to appropriately authorized staff.

The system must distinguish between identifying the same patient and granting access to that patient's records.

**Identifying the patient must not automatically grant every hospital unrestricted access to every medical record.**

## Access-control rules

- Verify the patient's identity.
- Apply applicable consent, privacy, and legal requirements.
- Restrict access to authorized hospitals and personnel.
- Support revocation or changes to access where applicable.
- Record relevant access events in audit logs.
- Protect especially sensitive information through appropriate additional controls.
- Make unavailable records clearly distinguishable from records that do not exist, without revealing protected information to unauthorized users.
- Allow a properly authorized emergency-access process where legally appropriate, with justification and auditing.

Design the application so cross-hospital sharing can be implemented through an appropriate authorization model rather than assuming all hospitals automatically share all records.

## Database considerations

Design appropriate relationships for:

- Global patient identity, if the legal and privacy model permits it.
- Hospital-specific patient registration.
- Hospital-specific visits.
- Clinical records.
- Authorized cross-hospital sharing.
- Consent or other legal authorization records, where required.
- Access logs.

If global patient identity cannot appropriately be shared, design a secure identity-matching and record-linking process consistent with applicable requirements.

Do not assume that the database alone can determine whether sharing is legally authorized.

# 9. COMPLETE PATIENT JOURNEY: HOSPITAL ENTRY TO EXIT

Implement the following patient workflow.

## STEP 1: Patient arrives

The patient arrives at the hospital.

The Receptionist opens the reception dashboard.

The system records arrival and identifies whether the patient is expected for an appointment or is a walk-in patient.

Emergency patients must be directed to the appropriate emergency process without unnecessary administrative delays.

## STEP 2: Search for or register the patient

The Receptionist searches for the patient.

If the patient already exists:
- Retrieve the existing patient profile.
- Verify identity.
- Show authorized historical information.
- Create a new visit for the current attendance.

If the patient is new:
- Register the patient.
- Generate a unique patient number.
- Save the patient profile.
- Create a new visit.

Prevent duplicate patient profiles.

## STEP 3: Register the visit

Create a visit record linked to:
- Patient.
- Hospital.
- Visit date and time.
- Appointment, if applicable.
- Visit type.
- Responsible department.
- Initial status.

The patient profile and visit must be separate database records.

## STEP 4: Triage

An authorized triage professional records the patient's initial assessment.

Possible information:
- Vital signs.
- Symptoms.
- Pain assessment.
- Relevant medical history.
- Allergies.
- Triage priority.
- Immediate care requirements.

Emergency cases must follow the hospital's emergency protocol.

## STEP 5: Doctor consultation

The doctor opens the patient's current visit.

The doctor can review relevant historical information they are authorized to access.

The doctor records:
- Presenting complaint.
- History of present illness.
- Relevant medical history.
- Examination findings.
- Clinical assessment.
- Diagnosis.
- Treatment plan.
- Follow-up instructions.

The system must record the responsible clinician and relevant timestamps.

## STEP 6: Determine whether tests are required

The doctor decides whether laboratory tests or other investigations are necessary.

If tests are not required, the workflow can proceed to treatment.

If tests are required:
1. Create a laboratory request.
2. Link the request to the patient and current visit.
3. Record the requested tests.
4. Send the request to the laboratory queue.

A laboratory request must not be created without the required authorization.

## STEP 7: Laboratory processing

The laboratory staff:
1. Receive the request.
2. Confirm the patient and requested tests.
3. Collect or receive the specimen.
4. Record specimen identification.
5. Process the specimen.
6. Record results.
7. Validate results when authorized.
8. Release approved results.
9. Record critical-result alerts when necessary.

Maintain result history and audit trails.

Unvalidated results must not be presented as validated final results.

## STEP 8: Doctor reviews results

The doctor receives a notification or sees the completed results in the patient record.

The doctor reviews the results and records the clinical interpretation and any changes to the treatment plan.

If additional tests are needed, the doctor can create new authorized requests.

If tests are not required or are already complete, continue to treatment.

## STEP 9: Treatment and prescription

The doctor records the treatment plan.

If medication is required:
1. Create a prescription.
2. Link it to the patient and current visit.
3. Record medicine, dosage, frequency, duration, and other required instructions.
4. Send the prescription to the pharmacy queue.

If medication is not required, the patient may continue to the next step.

Only authorized clinicians may create or modify prescriptions according to the permission rules.

## STEP 10: Pharmacy dispensing

The Pharmacist:
1. Opens the prescription.
2. Confirms its validity.
3. Checks stock and expiration dates.
4. Records the quantity dispensed.
5. Updates inventory using appropriate database transactions.
6. Records partial or complete dispensing.
7. Provides authorized dispensing information.

The system must prevent invalid stock deductions and maintain inventory history.

If medication is unavailable, record the appropriate status and follow the hospital's approved process.

## STEP 11: Billing and manual payments

Create an invoice for applicable services.

Possible charges include:
- Consultation.
- Laboratory tests.
- Procedures.
- Medicines.
- Other authorized services.

The Accountant can:
- Review invoice items.
- Record manual payments.
- Record partial payments.
- Calculate the outstanding balance.
- Issue receipts.
- Record authorized refunds and adjustments.

Supported payment methods may include cash, bank transfer, or another manually recorded method.

**Do not integrate online payment gateways.**

A payment record must not be treated as proof of an online transaction. It represents a payment recorded and verified through the hospital's approved manual process.

Clinical care and emergency treatment must not be unnecessarily delayed by financial processing.

## STEP 12: Doctor completes the care plan

The doctor ensures that:
- Relevant results have been reviewed.
- The treatment plan is documented.
- Required prescriptions are recorded.
- Follow-up instructions are documented.
- The patient receives appropriate instructions.

## STEP 13: Discharge or visit completion

Before completing the visit, verify the requirements applicable to that type of visit.

These may include:
- Clinical documentation.
- Review of required test results.
- Treatment instructions.
- Prescription instructions.
- Follow-up plan.
- Required administrative checks.

Do not force every patient to complete every workflow step. Some patients do not need laboratory tests, prescriptions, or additional procedures.

An authorized user completes the visit according to hospital policy.

## STEP 14: Patient exits

The system records:
- Visit completion or discharge time.
- Responsible staff member.
- Final visit status.
- Follow-up requirements.
- Relevant discharge instructions.
- Outstanding administrative items where appropriate.

The patient can leave the hospital.

The visit becomes part of the patient's historical record.

The patient's profile remains available for future visits.

# 20. TESTING REQUIREMENTS

Use Postman for API testing and an appropriate automated testing framework for backend and frontend tests.

## Patient management tests
- Register a new patient.
- Search for an existing patient.
- Retrieve patient history.
- Update permitted patient details.
- Reject invalid input.
- Prevent duplicate registration.
- Handle multiple possible identity matches.

## Returning-patient tests
1. Register a patient.
2. Record the first visit.
3. Add consultation notes, laboratory results, and a prescription.
4. Register a second visit for the same patient.
5. Confirm that the same patient profile is reused.
6. Confirm that the second visit is a new record.
7. Confirm that the first visit remains unchanged.
8. Confirm that authorized users can retrieve relevant historical records.
9. Confirm that unauthorized users cannot access restricted records.

## Cross-hospital tests
- A hospital can access its authorized records.
- A hospital cannot access another hospital's restricted records.
- Authorized cross-hospital sharing works as designed.
- Unauthorized sharing is rejected.
- Changing a hospital ID in a request does not bypass authorization.
- Patient identity matching does not automatically grant clinical-record access.

## Role-permission tests
- Receptionist cannot modify prescriptions.
- Pharmacist cannot modify clinical diagnoses.
- Laboratory Technician cannot perform unauthorized financial operations.
- Accountant cannot modify restricted clinical notes.
- Patient cannot access another patient's records.
- Hospital Manager cannot automatically access every hospital.
- Super Admin privileges are explicitly controlled.

## Clinical workflow tests
- A visit can proceed without laboratory tests when they are unnecessary.
- A visit can proceed without a prescription when medication is unnecessary.
- Laboratory results cannot be treated as validated before the required validation.
- Discharge or completion follows the appropriate workflow.
- Emergency handling does not depend on unnecessary administrative steps.

## Pharmacy tests
- Valid dispensing updates stock correctly.
- Expired medicines cannot be dispensed.
- Partial dispensing is recorded accurately.
- Concurrent stock operations do not produce incorrect quantities.

## Financial tests
- Invoice totals are correct.
- Partial payments update the balance correctly.
- Receipts match recorded payments.
- Unauthorized refunds are rejected.
- Financial history cannot be silently deleted.
- Payment recording does not accidentally duplicate a transaction.

## Security tests
- SQL injection attempts are safely handled.
- Unauthorized requests are rejected.
- Patient ownership checks work.
- Tenant isolation works.
- Sensitive information is not returned to unauthorized users.
- Invalid status transitions are rejected.
